### 1. Definition
The Blue Team is the defensive pillar under the CISO, comprising the SOC (monitoring/triage), CIRT (advanced incident response), and specialized units (DFIR, Threat Intel). SOCs operate either as an **Internal SOC** (dedicated to one company's environment) or an **MSSP** (Managed Security Service Provider, monitoring multiple external clients).

### 2. Why defenders care
Your environment dictates your visibility and your blind spots. Internal SOC analysts have deep business context but see a narrow threat landscape. MSSP analysts see a massive variety of attacks across multiple industries but lack the business context to easily spot subtle insider threats. Knowing where you sit determines your escalation paths, SLA pressures, and how you tune your SIEM.

### 3. How it works
*   **Hierarchy:** The CISO aligns security with business goals (e.g., hospital = patient safety). GRC ensures compliance (PCI-DSS, ISO 27001). Red Team attacks. Blue Team defends.
*   **Internal SOC:** Ingests logs from the company's specific stack (e.g., your FortiGates, on-prem AD). Rules are highly customized to the company's specific workflows and user behaviors.
*   **MSSP:** Uses a multi-tenant SIEM. Logs from 50 clients are ingested, tagged with a `tenant_id`, and normalized into a strict common schema (like Splunk CIM or Elastic ECS) so a single detection rule can run across all clients simultaneously.
*   **CIRT (CSIRT/CERT):** The "SWAT" team. They don't look at SIEM dashboards; they do memory forensics, reverse engineer malware, and handle legal chain-of-custody during massive breaches.

### 4. Key terms
*   **CISO:** Chief Information Security Officer (Executive leader).
*   **GRC:** Governance, Risk, and Compliance (Policy and audit team).
*   **MSSP:** Managed Security Service Provider (Outsourced SOC).
*   **Multi-tenant SIEM:** A SIEM architecture that logically separates data from different clients/customers.
*   **Dwell Time:** The time an attacker remains undetected in the network.

### 5. Attacker view + MITRE ATT&CK IDs
Attackers view MSSPs as high-value targets. Breaching an MSSP's management console gives them a "skeleton key" to all downstream clients.
*   **Supply Chain Compromise (T1195.002):** Attackers compromise the MSSP's software or remote management tools (like Kaseya or SolarWinds) to push malware to all clients simultaneously.
*   **Valid Accounts (T1078):** In an Internal SOC, attackers steal an IT admin's credentials. Because the internal SOC knows the admin's normal behavior, the attacker must perfectly mimic the admin's hours and access patterns to avoid triggering an alert.

### 6. Defender view: logs, detection, response
*   **Internal SOC Logs:** Heavily reliant on Active Directory and HR feeds. Detection relies on context (e.g., `Event ID 4624` for a user who is currently on PTO, or an admin accessing the CEO's email).
*   **MSSP Logs:** Rely on strict data normalization. If Client A uses Palo Alto firewalls and Client B uses FortiGate, the MSSP must map both to a common `firewall_traffic` dataset. Data segregation (tenant IDs) is critical to prevent cross-contamination.
*   **Detection:** In an MSSP, a rule might trigger if *any* client sees a rare process execution. In an Internal SOC, a rule triggers if *our specific* finance server talks to an unknown IP.

### 7. Mini scenario (attack > detect > respond)
*   **Attack:** An attacker compromises a widely used RMM (Remote Monitoring and Management) tool managed by an MSSP.
*   **Detect:** An MSSP L1 analyst notices the exact same suspicious PowerShell payload executing across 12 different client environments within a 5-minute window.
*   **Respond:** The analyst realizes this isn't 12 isolated incidents, but a single supply chain attack. They escalate immediately to CIRT. CIRT isolates the RMM management servers, revokes the compromised vendor certificates, and pushes an emergency block rule to all client firewalls.

### 8. Sigma/SPL/KQL sample
*Scenario: Detecting a single IP address authenticating to multiple different tenant environments (Potential MSSP console compromise or compromised MSP admin).*

*Microsoft Sentinel KQL:*
```kusto
SigninLogs
| where ResultType == 0 // Successful login
| summarize TenantCount = dcount(TenantId), Tenants = make_set(TenantId) by IPAddress
| where TenantCount > 1
```

### 9. Free tools
*   **MISP (Malware Information Sharing Platform):** Open-source threat intelligence sharing, heavily used by MSSPs and national CERTs to share IOCs.
*   **MITRE ATT&CK Navigator:** For mapping out your Blue Team's coverage against known adversary tactics.
*   **Security Onion:** A free, open-source platform for threat hunting and enterprise security monitoring (great for building an internal SOC lab).

### 10. Lab idea
*   **Build a Multi-Tenant SIEM:** Spin up an ELK stack (Elasticsearch, Logstash, Kibana) or Splunk Free. Create 3 separate VMs representing 3 different "clients." Ingest their Windows Event Logs into your SIEM, but ensure you tag them with `tenant:clientA`, `tenant:clientB`, etc. Write a detection rule that only alerts if a specific attack (like a brute force) hits *two or more* tenants simultaneously.

### 11. Common mistakes
*   **MSSP Context Blindness:** Closing a True Positive because "the client's admin probably ran this script," without actually verifying with the client.
*   **Internal Complacency:** Assuming "we are too small to be targeted" and ignoring low-fidelity alerts, allowing attackers to establish long-term dwell time.
*   **Poor Data Segregation:** In an MSSP, failing to properly tag logs with tenant IDs, leading to cross-contamination where Client A's alerts trigger on Client B's data.

### 12. 5-bullet recap
1. The Blue Team operates under the CISO and includes the SOC (triage), CIRT (advanced response), and specialized roles (DFIR, Threat Intel).
2. Internal SOCs offer deep business context but a narrow threat view; MSSPs offer high attack volume but lack business context.
3. MSSPs are prime targets for Supply Chain Compromise (T1195.002) because breaching them compromises all downstream clients.
4. Multi-tenant SIEM architecture and strict data normalization (CIM/ECS) are the backbone of MSSP operations.
5. CIRT is the "SWAT" team called in when the SOC is overwhelmed or a major breach requires forensics and reverse engineering.

### 13. 3 test questions
1. If an attacker compromises the software update mechanism of an MSP to push malware to all its clients, which MITRE ATT&CK technique are they utilizing?
2. In an MSSP environment, what architectural requirement is absolutely critical in the SIEM to ensure Client A's security analysts cannot see Client B's raw logs?
3. Which Blue Team sub-department is primarily responsible for handling the legal chain-of-custody, memory forensics, and reverse engineering during a massive breach?

***
**Answers:**
*1. Supply Chain Compromise (T1195.002). 2. Multi-tenancy / strict data segregation via tenant IDs. 3. CIRT (Cyber Incident Response Team) / CSIRT.*
